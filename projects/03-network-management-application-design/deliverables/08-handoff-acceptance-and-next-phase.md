## Design release and implementation handoff
This is a design release. Cutover, live-data migration and production rollback belong to a future implementation phase and are not claimed as delivered.

Handoff includes versioned prototype; journey and content specifications; component and state inventory; requirement-to-test traceability; research method and limitations; accessibility findings; feasibility constraints; prioritized backlog and deferred requests; acceptance record and receiving owner.

Product Owner accepts business design. QA records finding disposition. Technical Lead confirms receipt and outstanding implementation questions. PM assigns follow-up actions and closes the design initiative only after receiving-team acceptance. A design file sent by email is not sufficient handover evidence.

If final acceptance fails, hold handoff, preserve the prior accepted prototype version, record remaining findings and replan correction with the Product Owner. A future implementation charter should include infrastructure, integration, privacy, production QA, deployment, monitoring and support; none is implied by this prototype.

## Benefits and lessons
Task success is a design measure; production adoption, support calls and completion rates require measurement after implementation by the Product Owner. Establish baselines before attributing improvement. W16 scenario closure reports a completed design pack and CAD 116,000 estimated final spend, not business savings.

Lessons: task-level evidence avoids misleading averages; research must precede irreversible build commitments; Product Owner retains priority authority; a critical finding cannot be traded away for a Green status; design and live-service acceptance have different evidence needs.
