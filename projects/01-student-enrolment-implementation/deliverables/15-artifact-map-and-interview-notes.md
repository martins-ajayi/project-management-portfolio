# 15 | Artifact map and defensible interview narrative

## Follow the evidence chain

**Business need** → [Business case](01-business-case.md) → **authority and boundaries** → [Charter](02-project-charter.md) → **commitments** → [Scope/WBS](03-scope-wbs-and-schedule.md) and [RTM](../registers/requirements_traceability.csv) → **execution evidence** → [Milestone register](../registers/milestones.csv) and [UAT](../registers/uat_cases.csv) → **exception control** → [RAID](../registers/raid.csv), [W16 report](13-week-16-executive-status.md), [CHG-01](14-change-request-and-decision.md) → **release** → [Cutover](11-release-and-hypercare.md) → **closure** → [Benefits handoff](12-handover-and-benefits.md).

## Useful interview prompts (NOT factual talking points until validated)

- What trade-offs did you consider when deciding between a configurable platform and tailored implementation?
- What role did you personally play in setting scope, negotiating resources, and securing approvals?
- Tell me about a real requirement or migration issue you encountered: what was the evidence, who decided, and what changed?
- How did you know UAT was complete? What were the actual exit criteria, if known?
- What happened after deployment, and who was responsible for measuring benefits?

## Ethical presentation

Martins can accurately say: “This portfolio case study is a **reconstruction of the kind of implementation work shown in my experience**, with illustrative controls and scenarios to demonstrate how I approach delivery.” He should **not** say: “I personally controlled a CAD 240k budget, delivered 50 schools or resolved this particular defect” unless separately verified. The scenario never names organizations or uses client records.
